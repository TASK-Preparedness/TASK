## What you may do without asking

1. **Redistribute unmodified T.A.S.K.** (source or the project's official
   packages, unchanged) under the T.A.S.K. name, including for a fee, as long as
   you don't suggest you are the official source or are endorsed by the project.
2. **Refer to T.A.S.K. truthfully**: in articles, reviews, tutorials, talks,
   package repositories and lists of compatible software. Examples: "runs on
   T.A.S.K.", "based on T.A.S.K.", "a fork of T.A.S.K.", "compatible with
   T.A.S.K.", "T.A.S.K. training by <you>".
3. **Use the name inside the code.** Keep existing file names, package names
   (such as `task-core`), import paths, copyright notices and "About" text as
   the GPL and good practice require. Keeping the attribution notices is
   never a trademark violation.
4. **Install unmodified T.A.S.K. on hardware you sell**, and say so
   factually ("comes with T.A.S.K. preinstalled, unmodified, from the official
   release"). You may not call it an *official* or *certified* T.A.S.K. kit
   (see below).

## What needs a different name

If you **distribute a modified version** (a fork, a rebuilt package with
changes, a customized image), you must:

* give it a name and logo that are **not** T.A.S.K., "Tactical Access Support
  Kit", or confusingly similar;
* remove or replace the T.A.S.K. logo and the T.A.S.K. name from user-visible
  product branding (title bars, splash screens, desktop entries, app-store
  listings, web pages), while **keeping** all copyright and license notices;
* you may still say truthfully that it is "based on T.A.S.K." or "a fork of
  T.A.S.K.".

Small changes that only package or configure an unchanged release for a
distribution (a path, a dependency name, a security backport) are fine
under the T.A.S.K. name if you say so in your release notes.
